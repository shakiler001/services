Where school software teams need help

Your system has run for years and schools depend on it. The pressure now is to do more with it, faster, without breaking what works.

Audit and readiness check

A big new customer, a new state or country, a security questionnaire, or a busy season ahead, and nobody has looked at the whole system in years.

What I do: a clear report on performance, security and student-data access, with a ranked fix list.

Migration and modernization

Core modules still run on ASP.NET WebForms or MVC 5, deployments are manual, and hiring people who want to work on it is hard.

What I do: move it to modern .NET one module at a time, with tests first, while schools keep using it.

Custom modules and per-school changes

Every school or district wants something different: class routines and timetables, exam seat plans, report card formats, fee and scholarship rules.

What I do: build them as configurable modules, so one codebase serves every school without a fork per customer.

A website for every school on your platform

Each school wants its own website with its own look, showing live data from your product. Building one site at a time doesn't scale, and the platform has no clean way to feed them.

What I do: learn your code and data, build or extend the API, and create a website system where each school gets its own site from one codebase.

AI features on your own data

Schools ask what AI your product offers. Staff want answers from their own records, and teachers want help with report-card comments.

What I do: add AI that answers only from data each user is allowed to see, shows its source and is tested before release.

Student data and analytics

The data is all there, but principals and districts still export to Excel to see attendance trends, grade patterns or who is falling behind.

What I do: dashboards, early-warning flags and reporting exports built on the data you already hold.

Questions school software teams ask

Have you worked with US, UK or Canadian school systems?

I've built and run school systems that serve many schools on one platform, including English-medium schools with their own curricula and report cards: student records, results, admissions, fees, HR and reporting. The needs are the same everywhere. Your reporting and privacy rules (state reporting, FERPA, the UK school census, UK GDPR) I learn as part of the work, and I'll tell you up front where I'll need your team's knowledge.

Can you build inside our existing product?

Yes. I work in your repositories, your stack and your release process. ASP.NET WebForms, MVC, Web API, modern .NET and SQL Server are where I spend most of my time, and new modules follow the patterns your team already uses. If there's no API for what you need yet, I read your code and build one that websites and apps can use safely.

Is AI safe to use with student data?

It can be, if it's built carefully. AI features only see the records the signed-in user is already allowed to see, every answer shows where it came from, nothing is sent to outside services without your approval, and a fixed set of test questions is rerun before each release.

Will our schools notice the work?

No. Changes go to a test copy first and go live outside your busy dates, with a way back if anything looks wrong.

How do you handle student data?

I work inside your environment with the access you give me, use test or anonymized data where I can, sign your NDA and data agreements, and hand back access when the work ends.

What about time zones?

I'm in Dhaka. My working hours overlap US Eastern mornings and UK afternoons. Calls are booked in your hours, and written updates arrive before your day starts.

How do we start?

Tell me what your product does, what it's built with and what your team can't get to. I reply within one working day, and you get a fixed quote in writing within two working days. Fixed-price work is 50% upfront and 50% on delivery. Small tasks can also be done hourly at $45 an hour.
